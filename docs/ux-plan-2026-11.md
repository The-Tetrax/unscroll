# UI and UX plan, November 2026

Written 2026-11-18 against commit `3cde874`. It records what was audited, what the evidence says, what shipped with this document, and what is left.

## How the audit was done

1. Captured 48 screens of the production build with `node scripts/capture-journeys.mjs`: every tab and every reader step, at 390 x 844 (phone) and 1280 x 900 (desktop).
2. Looked at seven of the phone captures (Today, Explore, Recall and four lesson steps) and read the source of all five tabs and the lesson reader in `src/components`, `src/App.css` and `src/tokens.css`.
3. Looked up evidence for each gap. Every source below carries a label saying how much of it I read.
4. Built the changes, then captured 50 screens again with a dark device setting (`--dark`) and ran the browser tests.

The app was already in good shape: semantic markup, 44 px targets, safe-area padding, reduced motion, and axe scans with no violations. The gaps were about comfort while reading, not about broken screens.

### Source labels

- **Read**: I fetched the page in this session and the claim is on it.
- **Abstract**: I read the paper's abstract only (Europe PMC or Crossref).
- **Machine summary**: I read only Semantic Scholar's generated one-line summary. Treat as a pointer, not a finding.
- **Secondary**: a summary site, not the original study.
- **Not re-read**: cited from prior knowledge of a standard. I did not open it in this session.

## Shipped with this plan

### 1. Dark theme that follows the device

**Evidence.**

- Nielsen Norman Group, "Dark Mode vs. Light Mode: Which Is Better?" (Budiu, 2020). **Read.** For people with normal vision, dark text on a light background gives better reading performance; the article cites Piepenbrock et al. 2013 and Dobres et al. 2017. Some people with low vision, such as cataract, do better with light text on dark. Its recommendation: "we strongly recommend that designers allow users to switch to dark mode", and it names long-form reading apps.
- Nielsen Norman Group, "Dark Mode: How Users Think About It and Issues to Avoid" (Kohler and Zhang, 2023). **Read.** Users treat dark mode as a device-level setting and expect apps to mirror it. An in-app switch is acceptable for reading apps. Listed pitfalls: dividers and cards that disappear, depth shown with shadows where lighter surfaces are needed, saturated colors, images with opaque light backgrounds, and contrast below 4.5:1 for text or 3:1 for controls.
- web.dev, "prefers-color-scheme" and "color-scheme". **Read.** Follow the system by default with a manual override, set the `color-scheme` property so native controls match, avoid pure white text on a dark background, keep colors in semantic variables, and dim images slightly.
- Piepenbrock et al. 2013, "Positive display polarity is advantageous for both younger and older adults", Ergonomics. **Machine summary.** Consistent with keeping light as the result on a light device.

**What changed.**

- `src/tokens.css` gains a `:root[data-theme='dark']` block of 28 tokens. Paper is `#1a1617`, not black. Ink is `#f1ece4`, not white. Raised surfaces are lighter (`#262021`, `#362e2f`), which is how depth is shown without shadows. The red accent becomes `#f47983` so it reaches 4.5:1 as text on dark paper, and buttons switch to dark text on that red.
- Four new tokens hold colors that must not flip: `--color-inverse` and `--color-on-inverse` for the hero and one path tile, `--color-mascot-paper` and `--color-mascot-ink` for the fox. The hero stays a dark panel with the red field in both themes.
- The ten topic colors become deep tints in the dark theme, so the takeaway box and practice box are not bright blocks.
- The choice is an optional `theme` field (`system`, `light`, `dark`) in the version-1 library. Absent means Match device. Old backups import unchanged.
- `public/theme.js` runs in the document head and sets the theme before the first paint, from a `localStorage` hint or the device setting. The saved library stays the source of truth; the hint only prevents a flash when the saved choice differs from the device.
- `html { color-scheme }` and the `theme-color` meta follow the theme, so native controls, scrollbars and the browser toolbar match.
- Controls: **You > Preferences > Appearance**, and the reading settings inside a lesson.

**How it is checked.** `tests/theme.test.ts` parses the token file and computes WCAG contrast for 33 text pairs and 6 boundary pairs in each theme (78 contrast cases), and checks the theme resolution table, the backup round trip, and that `public/theme.js` uses the same key and colors as the app. `tests/e2e/comfort.spec.ts` runs axe on all five screens and four reader states with a dark device in three browser profiles. It also records every value the theme attribute takes, starting before the first page script, so a wrong theme at any moment fails the test. That covers the attribute, not the pixels on a real screen.

**Limits.**

- The 604 diagrams are drawn on light paper (469 on `#f7f4ed`, 114 on white, 21 on two other ivories). In the dark theme they stay light cards, dimmed to 88 % brightness. This is the "opaque background" pitfall in the NN/g article and it is item 11.
- The install manifest has one `background_color` and one `theme_color`, both ivory, so I expect the installed app's splash screen to be ivory before the dark theme appears. Not checked on a phone.
- `/api/update.html` has its own stylesheet and stays light.

### 2. Reading settings inside the lesson

**Evidence.** The audit: changing text size needed four steps away from the text (close the lesson, open You, find Preferences, reopen the lesson). The NN/g 2023 article accepts an in-app theme switch for reading apps. **Read.** For size, Rello et al., "Make It Big! The Effect of Font Size and Line Spacing on Online Readability" (CHI 2016): **Machine summary** only, which says readability rose with font size and recommends size 18 or larger for text-heavy sites. I have not read the paper, so I do not know its unit or viewing distance, and I did not change the default sizes (17 px and 20 px) on that basis.

**What changed.** A **Reading settings** button in the lesson toolbar opens a panel with **Text size** (Standard text, Larger text) and **Appearance** (Match device, Light, Dark). It writes the same two fields as the profile. It shows no "saved" message, because the app reports saved settings only after storage confirms; a storage failure still appears in the lesson's existing alert. The panel exists in the page only while it is open.

**Cost.** The toolbar now has four buttons, and the fourth takes 48 px from the topic name beside them. To give 16 px back, the toolbar gap on phones is 8 px where it was 16 px. Measured after that at 320, 360, 384, 390, 393 and 412 px: all four buttons stay 44 px with a 12 px margin and the toolbar stays 61 px tall. The longest topic name, "Cinema & photography", needs 158.5 px on one line. It fits from 384 px up and wraps to two lines at 360 px and 320 px. Before this change it fit at 360 px, with 168 px available, and already wrapped at 320 px.

**How it is checked.** One browser test changes both settings inside a lesson, checks the paragraph size (17 px to 20 px), the dialog background, the stored library, the 320 px layout, and that the profile shows the same choices. One existing assertion changed with the design: `tests/e2e/iteration.spec.ts` counted three toolbar buttons and two footer buttons, and now counts four and two.

### 3. Position within a step

**Evidence.** Laws of UX, "Goal-Gradient Effect". **Secondary.** Its takeaway: "Provide a clear indication of progress in order to motivate users to complete tasks". It points to Hull (1932) and Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng (2006), which I did not read. The existing step tabs show which of three steps you are on; nothing showed how far through a step you were.

**What changed.** A 3 px bar above the lesson footer fills as the step is scrolled. It is hidden when the step fits on screen, has no animation, and is `aria-hidden`: the step tabs already expose the current step to assistive technology, and a scroll-driven value would add noise.

### 4. Explore: fewer path tiles before the lessons

**Evidence.** Measured in the capture: 22 tiles in two columns are 11 rows of 180 px, about 2,100 px, before "The whole collection". The grid now shows six tiles on phones (three rows, about 560 px) and eight on wider screens, then a **Show all 22 paths** button with `aria-expanded`. The path you follow is moved to the front so it is never among the hidden ones. One existing browser test opened a path tile that is now behind the button; it presses **Show all 22 paths** first.

I did not fetch a source for this one. It is a layout measurement, and the hidden tiles are one tap away.

### 5. In progress label

**Evidence.** Laws of UX, "Zeigarnik Effect". **Secondary.** From prior knowledge, not re-read: later attempts to reproduce the original memory finding have been inconsistent. So I used it only to justify a label, not a mechanic. The practical gap: Vault and Today already list unfinished bites, but a card in Explore gave no sign that a position was saved.

**What changed.** A card shows **In progress** when the lesson has a saved position and is not complete. Opening and closing a lesson without moving past step one saves nothing and shows nothing. No XP, no reminder.

### 6 to 8. Smaller fixes

- **Step labels 12 px.** They were 11 px at phone widths. A browser test checks the size and that all three labels fit at 320 px.
- **Bold current tab.** WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.1, Use of Color. **Not re-read.** The current tab already had `aria-current` and a heavier icon stroke; the label is now bold as well.
- **No pull-to-refresh in the installed app.** Chrome for Developers, "Take control of your scroll: customizing pull-to-refresh and overflow effects". **Read.** `overscroll-behavior-y: contain` on the viewport element disables Chrome's pull-to-refresh, and the article names a PWA as the case for turning it off. The rule applies only inside `@media (display-mode: standalone)`, so a browser tab keeps the gesture. A reload in the middle of a lesson loses the scroll position and gains nothing, because updates arrive through the in-app prompt. I have not confirmed on a phone that the installed app shows the gesture today; if it does not, the rule has no effect.

## Next

### 9. Restore the scroll position of an unfinished lesson

A draft stores the step, the selected answer and whether feedback was shown. It does not store how far the step was scrolled, so resuming an 800-word step starts at its top. Plan: an optional `position` number from 0 to 1 on `ReaderDraft`, written when the lesson closes or the page is hidden, applied once after the diagrams have loaded. It touches the strict schema, `saveReaderDraft`, and the backup tests, which is why it is not in this change.

### 10. Wording when the streak is zero

Silverman and Barasch, "On or Off Track: How (Broken) Streaks Affect Consumer Decisions", Journal of Consumer Research, 2022. **Abstract.** Across seven studies, showing an intact streak raised later engagement compared with showing a broken one, and the effect came from how the log represented the behavior, not from the behavior itself.

After any missed day, Today shows **0 day streak**. That is a broken streak made visible. A new reader sees the same zero before the first lesson. Plan: show the count only when it is one or more, and neutral text otherwise. The recall step already says reviews are "spaced by recall, not by a streak deadline", so this fits the app's existing stance.

## Later

### 11. Dark variants of the diagrams

Each SVG has an opaque light rectangle and dark strokes. Options: generate a second set, or draw with CSS variables and inline the SVGs. Both change `scripts/generate-teaching-assets.ts`, the 604 files, and their hash and licence records in `public/asset-licenses.json`. It should be its own change with its own visual review.

### 12. Update-recovery page

`public/api/update.css` is separate on purpose, so that a broken app bundle cannot break recovery. It can copy the dark tokens without sharing a file.

## Undecided

### 13. More text sizes

Two sizes exist. A third, or a line-spacing control, needs the Rello et al. paper read in full first. Pinch zoom is not disabled, so WCAG 2.2 criterion 1.4.4 (Resize Text, **not re-read**) can already be met by zooming.

### 14. Topic chips on a phone

Eleven chips wrap to four rows. A single scrolling row would save about 150 px and leave about seven topics off screen. I have no evidence either way for this app, so it stays as it is.

## Ideas rejected on the evidence

- **Highlighting text in lessons.** Dunlosky et al., "Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques", Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 2013. **Abstract.** Highlighting and rereading received a low utility rating; practice testing and distributed practice received high ratings. The app already spends its effort on the two high-rated techniques.
- **Dark as the default for everyone, or switching by time of day.** The NN/g articles (**read**) and Piepenbrock et al. (**machine summary**) favor light for most readers and following the device setting.

## What already matches the evidence

These were checked during the audit and need no change.

| Existing behavior | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Every lesson ends with a recall question and a transfer question before completion | Roediger and Karpicke, "Test-Enhanced Learning", Psychological Science, 2006. **Abstract.** After two days and one week, students who took recall tests retained more than students who restudied, even though restudying had left them more confident. |
| First review after one day. A remembered item waits at least 3 days and then twice as long each time, or at least 7 days and 2.8 times as long after an easy recall, up to 365 days. A forgotten item returns the next day. | Cepeda et al., "Spacing Effects in Learning: A Temporal Ridgeline of Optimal Retention", Psychological Science, 2008. **Abstract.** With more than 1,350 participants, the best gap grew as the retention goal grew: about 20 to 40 % of a one-week delay, about 5 to 10 % of a one-year delay. The study used one gap before one test, so it supports widening the gaps as the goal moves out. It does not test the app's exact multipliers. |
| The daily queue is finite and is never refilled with completed lessons | Product rule in `docs/TEACHING.md` and the repository instructions, not a research claim. |
