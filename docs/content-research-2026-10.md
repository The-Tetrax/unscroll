## Most useful gaps to author first

- **Planning work that remains after delegation:** proposal 09 separates noticing, finding options, deciding, and checking. The existing care lesson recognizes overlooked work but does not give this task-level decomposition.
- **Information that a meeting never collects:** proposal 10 concerns facts distributed across people, rather than another lesson on listening or confidence.
- **Systems that create extra work:** proposals 05-07 distinguish delayed correction, displaced costs, and repeat requests caused by unfinished service. None needs a productivity promise.
- **Protection that does not depend on someone's goodwill:** proposal 11 adds an institutional question to the existing lessons on personal agency and fair rules.
- **Old-phone failure diagnosis and accessible delivery:** proposals 13, 14, and 16 add skew, resampling artifacts, and meaningful captions instead of presets or equipment shopping.
- **Drawing marks and UI behavior:** proposals 18-20 teach operations on paper; proposals 21-24 examine behavior that a polished screenshot cannot establish.

## Evidence and authoring boundaries

The citation ledger documents **26 distinct public source pages**, including three abstract-only pages, plus **two Crossref records containing publisher-deposited abstracts**. That is 28 retained source records. Search results, redirect aliases, bare citation metadata, blocked pages, and failed URLs are excluded from these counts. One additional checked research abstract is identified under rejected candidates and is not included in the retained-source total.

The sources support the named concepts, not the effectiveness of these newly proposed exercises. All practice timings, props, fictional scenarios, and numerical teaching examples below are original design choices. They are not study results or tested learning doses.

The 24 proposals were subsequently authored as the field-guide collection. Authoring adapted the invented subgroup counts and allowed 10 rather than 8 minutes for the two delayed-feedback runs. Those are exercise-design choices; the source claims and limitations are unchanged.

- Use `research` only when an authored lesson explains a scoped empirical finding. An abstract check does not establish methods or results absent from that abstract.
- Use `framework` for philosophical applications, practical diagnosis, and craft exercises. Use `reference` for statistical definitions and documented technical or accessibility requirements.
- Do not call a philosophical view a scientific finding. Do not turn camera or drawing conventions into universal laws of audience response.
- Practices use paper, ordinary household objects, and the phone or software already available. No purchase, installation, public upload, manual camera mode, log recording, or recent handset is required. A planning fallback is labeled as planning, not a completed edit.
- Use one's own material, fictional work examples, and voluntary participation. Keep private messages, faces, account information, and employer records out of shared work. No diagnosis, treatment advice, personality profiling, covert observation, or manipulation exercises.
- Write new explanations and retrieval questions. Link to sources; do not import their diagrams, photographs, transcripts, distinctive teaching examples, or exercise text. A public page is not permission to redistribute its contents.

## Pack A: Four checks before judging evidence

Everyday judgment, four lessons. The outputs are a selection check, a grouped comparison, a dated estimate, and a perceived-norm check.

### 01. Question the rebound

**Distinct skill:** Recognize regression toward the mean when a case receives attention because its first result was unusually extreme. Separate a subsequent less-extreme result from evidence that an intervention caused it.

**Practice, 6 minutes:** Use this fictional claim: a team received extra help after its worst week of late reports, and lateness fell the next week. Mark why that week was selected. Write two explanations for the fall, one involving help and one involving temporary variation. Specify an additional baseline or comparison that would help distinguish them. Keep the causal conclusion open.

**Source check:** S01, Bland and Altman, [Statistic Notes: Regression towards the mean](https://www.bmj.com/content/308/6942/1499), BMJ; S02, their [Some examples of regression towards the mean](https://www.bmj.com/content/309/6957/780), BMJ. Both article bodies were accessible. S02 explicitly discusses extreme selection and changes wrongly credited to treatment. The proposed example concerns office work, not treatment.

**Limitation:** This is an expectation under a model of variation and imperfect association, not a prediction that every bad week improves. Real interventions and changes in conditions can coexist with regression.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `thinking-007`, "Compare changes without assuming trends match," teaches a comparison design. This proposal teaches the selection mechanism that makes an extreme starting point misleading even before choosing that design.

### 02. Recount the mixed groups

**Distinct skill:** Identify an aggregate reversal caused by different mixtures of cases. Calculate subgroup and overall rates without assuming either comparison answers the causal question automatically.

**Practice, 10 minutes:** Calculate success rates for this invented work example. Method A completes 9 of 10 routine jobs and 30 of 50 difficult jobs. Method B completes 40 of 50 routine jobs and 5 of 10 difficult jobs. Compare each job type and then both totals. Write which method received more difficult work. Check: A has higher within-type rates, 90% and 60% versus 80% and 50%, but a lower overall rate, 65% versus 75%.

**Source check:** S03, [Simpson's Paradox](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/paradox-simpson/), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Sections 1-2 explain association reversals and weighting; sections 3.2-3.4 explain why causal roles matter when choosing a partition. Relevant body sections were read.

**Limitation:** "Always split the data" is not a valid remedy. Conditioning on a mediator or a selection variable can answer the wrong question. These invented rates establish arithmetic, not the causal superiority of either method.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `thinking-009`, "Find the denominator before comparing fault counts," corrects raw-count comparisons. Here both denominators are valid, yet differing case mixtures reverse the ranking. It also extends, rather than repeats, `thinking-002` on confounding.

### 03. Date the estimate

**Distinct skill:** Separate the period an estimate describes from the date and information set used to publish it. Distinguish a revision about the past from a new event in the present.

**Practice, 7 minutes:** Make two rows for a fictional attendance bulletin: Monday's estimate of last week's attendance is 40; Tuesday's estimate of that same week is 47 after late returns. Give each row a reference period, publication day, value, and revision reason. Rewrite "Attendance rose by seven today" so it describes what changed in the estimate instead.

**Source check:** S04, [Gross domestic product (GDP) QMI](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/methodologies/grossdomesticproductgdpqmi), UK Office for National Statistics. Section 5, "Accuracy and reliability," explains incomplete early data, later information, methodological revisions, and the accuracy/timeliness trade-off. The report is dated 8 April 2022; it is used for this methodological distinction, not current release schedules.

**Limitation:** A revision is neither proof of dishonesty nor an automatic improvement. Read the stated reason and check definitions. An unrevised series can still be inaccurate.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `thinking-034`, "Review a choice using what was knowable then," preserves a decision's information set. This proposal adds the specific reading skill of tracking multiple published values for one reference period.

### 04. Check the norm you imagine

**Distinct skill:** Distinguish your preference from your belief about other people's preferences. Recognize pluralistic ignorance as a possible group-level mismatch between private attitudes and the norm people think others endorse.

**Practice, 8 minutes:** Choose a harmless shared routine, such as a live check-in versus written notes. Make three columns: your preference, your estimate of the group's preference, and direct evidence for that estimate. Replace an unsupported "everyone wants this" with what is known. Draft a neutral, optional question that would collect each person's own preference rather than asking them to guess the group's. Do not send it or claim a group result as part of this drill.

**Source check:** S05, Prentice and Miller, [Pluralistic ignorance and alcohol use on campus: Some consequences of misperceiving the social norm](https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/0022-3514.64.2.243), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology / APA, 1993. The accessible abstract reports four studies comparing students' own attitudes with estimates of peers' attitudes and identifies a mismatch. The full paper was not reviewed. Only the norm-estimation distinction is used here, with no alcohol-use advice.

**Limitation:** The campus finding does not establish misperception in your workplace. A private guess, silence, or one dissenting voice cannot diagnose a whole group's beliefs. An actual check would need voluntary responses and attention to who feels safe answering; a small poll is not proof of a universal effect.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `perspective-020`, "Test the rule against an individual objection," already says not to treat silence as agreement. This proposal adds a specific distinction between the distribution of private preferences and people's beliefs about that distribution. It concerns perceived norms, unlike proposal 10's missing factual information.

## Pack B: Diagnose recurring shared work

Everyday systems, four lessons. Apply these to a low-stakes shared task, not a live production system or a person's health.

### 05. Allow for feedback delay

**Distinct skill:** Include actions whose effects have not arrived yet when making another correction. Identify overshoot caused by responding repeatedly to a delayed state.

**Practice, 8 minutes:** Use an invented paper model. A marker starts at 0; the target is 3. At the start of each round, apply the +1 or -1 command issued two rounds earlier. Then issue +1 if the marker is below target, -1 if above, or 0 if at target. Run six rounds. Repeat, but choose each command using the current position plus pending commands. Compare overshoot and keep the command queues visible.

**Source check:** S06, Donella Meadows, [Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/), Donella Meadows Project / Academy for Systems Change. Section 9 connects feedback delays with oscillation and discusses delay relative to system change. The final caution rejects treating the list as a universal recipe.

**Limitation:** The paper model assumes a fixed known delay and additive effects. Actual delays, disturbances, and limits differ. It does not justify waiting through an urgent safety problem or promise that slower action is always better.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `thinking-037`, "Trace the feedback created by retries," concerns reinforcing feedback. This is delayed corrective feedback. `life-010` keeps a follow-up date; it does not model pending corrections and overshoot.

### 06. Pair speed with a cost check

**Distinct skill:** Distinguish outcome, process, and balancing measures. Check whether improving a local target moves work or harm elsewhere.

**Practice, 8 minutes:** Draft a measurement card for shortening a recurring meeting. Specify its intended outcome, one observable process change, and a balancing measure such as clarification work needed after unresolved decisions. Define how to record that work without monitoring individuals secretly. Add one condition that would make the group revise the change.

**Source check:** S07, [Model for Improvement: Establishing Measures](https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement/establishing-measures), Institute for Healthcare Improvement. "Three Types of Measures" explicitly distinguishes outcome, process, and balancing measures. The page also distinguishes measurement for improvement from research.

**Limitation:** The meeting application is an original use of a quality-improvement framework. A few observations do not establish causation, and a convenient balancing measure can still omit another person's cost. Do not import healthcare outcome claims into office work.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `tech-037`, "Define an SLI denominator before reading its percentage," checks a measure's meaning and observation boundary. This proposal asks which other outcome must be measured because pursuing the first target can create a trade-off.

### 07. Count work caused by rework

**Distinct skill:** Identify failure demand: a new contact generated by an earlier failure to meet the original need. Separate it from a legitimate new request or necessary follow-up.

**Practice, 10 minutes:** Review one of your own non-sensitive service threads, or invent a five-contact example. Label each contact as original need, repeat caused by an unmet need, genuinely changed need, or uncertain. For one repeated contact, identify what information or completed action was missing earlier. Draft one upstream change instead of a target to answer messages faster.

**Source check:** S08, [Failure Demand](https://beyondcommandandcontrol.com/failure-demand/), Vanguard Consulting, the organization associated with John Seddon's framework. The definition and "Failure Demand Analysis" sections distinguish failure-generated contacts from ordinary service demand. This is a primary practitioner explanation with a commercial interest, not an independent intervention study.

**Limitation:** Do not repeat the page's prevalence or savings percentages as general facts. Not every repeat is avoidable, and reducing contact by making access harder is not success. Classify provisionally; do not blame the customer or individual worker.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `work-010`, "Include the upkeep in an automation proposal," evaluates the costs of automating recurring work. This proposal first asks whether the recurring work is being created unnecessarily and should be removed rather than automated.

### 08. Design rules for shared use

**Distinct skill:** Treat a shared resource as a governance problem with users, replenishment or availability, rules, and conflict resolution, rather than assuming either private ownership or goodwill settles it.

**Practice, 12 minutes:** Draft a card for a fictional shared art-supply cupboard. Name what is depleted, how replacement happens, who can use it, how users can check its state, and how a disputed rule can be changed. Test the card against a newcomer and a frequent user. If discussing a real cupboard, seek a voluntary review; do not impose the draft.

**Source check:** S09, [2009 Economic Sciences prize announcement](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2009/press-release/), Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences / NobelPrize.org, summarizes Ostrom's studies of user-managed resources and their decision and enforcement arrangements. S10, Ostrom, "A diagnostic approach for going beyond panaceas," PNAS / National Academy of Sciences: the [publisher-deposited abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1073/pnas.0702288104) identifies resource systems, resource units, users, governance, and context. The PNAS article body was not accessed.

**Limitation:** A cupboard is an introductory analogy, not a natural-resource field study. Neither source establishes that every community can self-govern successfully or that one checklist replaces law, funding, unequal power, and local knowledge.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `perspective-014`, "Choose a rule before knowing your position," tests fairness. This adds how a rule is maintained, observed, disputed, and revised around a resource that multiple people use.

## Pack C: Make work and objections visible

Social and philosophical worklife knowledge, four lessons. These concern shared arrangements and information, not decoding personalities.

### 09. Share the noticing work

**Distinct skill:** Separate the physical execution of a task from anticipating a need, finding options, deciding, and monitoring completion. Notice which parts still sit with the person supposedly delegating.

**Practice, 10 minutes:** Choose one ordinary shared task, such as arranging a group lunch. Make five rows: noticing the need, finding options, deciding, checking the result, and execution. Add columns for the current owner, missing information, and proposed owner. Mark unknowns. Propose transferring one complete, agreed responsibility with the necessary information and authority, rather than assigning only the final errand.

**Source check:** S11, Allison Daminger, [The Cognitive Dimension of Household Labor](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0003122419859007), American Sociological Review / SAGE. The public abstract reports 70 interviews with members of 35 couples and identifies the four cognitive components. Full article access was restricted.

**Limitation:** A qualitative household sample does not establish every household's division of work or a causal effect of this worksheet. The workplace transfer is an analogy. Ask the people involved instead of assigning burdens by gender or treating disagreement as bad character.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `perspective-018`, "Count receiving help as part of a human life," already recognizes care and overlooked coordination. This supplies an operational decomposition that distinguishes doing the task from retaining its planning and monitoring burden.

### 10. Collect facts before preferences

**Distinct skill:** Identify decision-relevant information held by only one participant before repeated shared facts and early preferences dominate the discussion.

**Practice, 10 minutes:** For a low-stakes group choice, such as selecting a workshop room, invite each willing person to write one relevant fact that others may not have, with its source or uncertainty. Collect and group the facts before asking for preferred rooms. Record one new fact and whether it changes a criterion. A solo fallback is to prepare this fact-collection sheet, without claiming a group result.

**Source check:** S12, Stasser and Titus, [Pooling of unshared information in group decision making](https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/0022-3514.48.6.1467), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology / APA. Its public abstract describes four-person groups and failures to combine distributed candidate information. S13, Lu, Yuan, and McLeod, "Twenty-Five Years of Hidden Profiles in Group Decision Making: A Meta-Analysis," Personality and Social Psychology Review / SAGE: the [deposited abstract](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177/1088868311417243) summarizes 65 studies and reports moderators. Neither full paper was reviewed.

**Limitation:** Unique information is not automatically accurate or important. Laboratory hidden-profile tasks differ from workplaces, and these sources do not validate this exact meeting format. Permit confidentiality and refusal; do not demand personal disclosures.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `observer-022`, "Create shared information before coordinating," aligns people on a plan. Here the group does not yet possess the information needed to choose the plan. The missing operation is pooling distributed facts, not acknowledgment.

### 11. Separate permission from protection

**Distinct skill:** Apply republican non-domination to distinguish being left alone today from having protection against another person's uncontrolled power tomorrow.

**Practice, 8 minutes:** Compare two fictional club-room policies. In one, a coordinator usually permits access but can cancel it without reasons or review. In the other, published criteria and an independent review route constrain cancellation. List what survives a change of coordinator. Draft one request for a reason, criterion, or appeal route, not a tactic for staying in favor.

**Source check:** S14, [Republicanism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/republicanism/), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Sections 1.2-1.3 distinguish non-domination from non-interference; section 2.2 discusses arbitrary power; section 4.2 discusses contestation. Relevant body sections were read.

**Limitation:** This is one contested philosophical account of freedom, not a legal test or an instruction to confront someone in an unsafe situation. Rules on paper are insufficient if people cannot use them. Delegated, accountable discretion is not automatically domination.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `perspective-001`, "Separate responsibility from control of results," locates personal agency. `perspective-020` tests objections to a rule. This proposal examines the structure of power over that rule, including cases where nobody has yet used it badly.

### 12. Record what the categories miss

**Distinct skill:** Ask whether an institution's vocabulary lets people describe relevant experiences, instead of discarding an account because it fits no existing label. Distinguish missing shared concepts from institutions ignoring concepts people already have.

**Practice, 9 minutes:** Use a fictional workshop feedback form with only "venue" and "content" categories. A new attendee reports being unable to participate because instructions used unexplained group shorthand. Preserve that description, add an open response route and a provisional category, and write how the attendee could correct your wording. Identify who decides whether the new category is retained.

**Source check:** S15, [Feminist Social Epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/feminist-social-epistemology/), Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Section 4.1 explains Fricker's hermeneutical injustice and critiques involving plural interpretive communities and contributory injustice. These are philosophical analyses, not experimental effects.

**Limitation:** A poor form or missing word alone does not establish hermeneutical injustice. That claim concerns unequal participation in shared interpretation and resulting disadvantage. People can already understand their experience while an institution refuses their account. Do not turn the exercise into a diagnosis of someone else's oppression.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `thinking-024`, "Keep the meaning of a key term fixed," prevents equivocation within an argument. This proposal asks who supplies the available terms and what those terms prevent the organization from hearing.

## Pack D: Phone footage, motion, and access

Creative cinema, four lessons. Use one's own footage, a supported phone, safe tabletop props, and ordinary lighting. Keep original recordings.

### 13. Separate skew from blur

**Distinct skill:** Distinguish rolling-shutter geometry from motion blur and camera shake. Recognize that different parts of a frame can represent different moments.

**Practice, 8 minutes:** Support the phone safely on a table. Record a card with a bold vertical line standing still, then move the card sideways at two comfortable speeds while the camera stays fixed. Compare paused frames for a leaning line and real-time playback for blur. Record "not visible in this test" if no skew appears; do not force a whip pan or use flashing lights.

**Source check:** S16, [Global & Rolling Shutters](https://www.reddigitalcinema.com/red-101/global-rolling-shutter), RED Digital Cinema. The "Rolling Shutter" section explains staggered capture and angled or sheared moving subjects. Relevant technical body text was accessible.

**Limitation:** Readout speed, stabilization, exposure, and processing vary by phone and mode. A short shutter time does not make readout simultaneous. The source's camera accessories and stylistic preferences are not recommendations for this curriculum.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `cinema-012`, "Choose frame rate for motion, not a cinema badge," distinguishes frame rate and shutter duration. `cinema-011` concerns steadiness and crop. Neither explains within-frame time differences and geometric skew.

### 14. Check detail after resizing

**Distinct skill:** Identify false repeating patterns introduced when fine image detail is resampled to a smaller output. Distinguish apparent texture from detail present in the subject.

**Practice, 9 minutes:** Photograph your own page of closely spaced parallel lines beside a coarser set. Compare the original at a detailed view with a fit-to-screen view. If an existing editor offers image-size export, compare one smaller copy too. Mark any new bands, missing lines, or unchanged areas. Record the viewing and export conditions; leave the responsible processing stage unknown if the comparison cannot isolate it. Keep a no-artifact result.

**Source check:** S17, [Image Resizing for the Web & Email](https://www.cambridgeincolour.com/tutorials/image-resize-for-web.htm), Cambridge in Colour, a specialist photography education site, not a university publication. "Background: Moire Artifacts" and the downsizing comparisons explain false patterns and the sharpness/aliasing trade-off.

**Limitation:** This source and exercise primarily concern resizing. They cannot locate an artifact in sensor capture, compression, or playback without separate comparisons. Do not prescribe old software settings from the article or deliberately blur important information without checking the result.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `cinema-008` compares cropping with moving the camera; `design-023` checks small text. This adds sampling-induced false detail in patterned clothes, drawings, title graphics, and stills used in a phone film.

### 15. Change spacing, keep duration

**Distinct skill:** Separate an animation's total duration from the distribution of object positions within it. Use spacing to make an object appear to slow down without changing the number of images.

**Practice, 15 minutes:** Mark two eight-position paths of equal length on scrap paper. One has equal gaps; the other has large early gaps that shrink toward the end. Photograph an ordinary counter at each position with the phone supported. If an existing editor can set still durations, play both sequences at the same duration per image. Otherwise compare the two position strips and label motion playback untested. Record which variable stayed fixed.

**Source check:** S18, [Stop motion animation explained](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/animation/discover/stop-motion-animation.html), Adobe, documents sequential stills, stable framing, lighting, and playback. S19, [Understanding the 12 principles of animation](https://www.adobe.com/creativecloud/animation/discover/principles-of-animation.html), Adobe, sections 6 and 9, discusses slow in/slow out and timing. Both relevant article bodies were accessible.

**Limitation:** These are craft references, not guarantees of realism or emotion. The eight-image exercise is deliberately small. Software support and capture consistency limit playback; manually swiping a gallery does not establish equal frame timing. No Adobe product is required.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `cinema-060`, "Test storyboard timing before the shoot," varies shot holds. This keeps sequence duration fixed while changing motion within the sequence, rather than making another timed storyboard.

### 16. Caption the sound that matters

**Distinct skill:** Translate necessary audio information into accurate, synchronized captions, including an offscreen sound or speaker distinction when it affects understanding.

**Practice, 12 minutes:** Record an original 15-second tabletop scene with one spoken sentence and a gentle offscreen knock. Replay it and write a timed caption plan containing the words and the meaningful knock. Check that each entry appears when the information becomes audible, not before. Add captions with existing controls if available; otherwise retain the timestamped plan and do not call the clip captioned.

**Source check:** S20, [Captions/Subtitles](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/captions/), W3C Web Accessibility Initiative. "Introduction," "Automatic Captions are Not Sufficient," and "Creating Captions" support speech, necessary non-speech information, synchronization, and manual accuracy checking.

**Limitation:** Captions do not replace audio description of visual-only information. Automatically generated text must be checked. Exact file and styling support vary by player; a readable plan does not demonstrate an accessible exported video.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `cinema-052`, "Mix for intelligibility with room for peaks," improves the audible mix. This preserves meaning for a viewer who cannot hear it, including information a dialogue-only transcript would omit.

## Pack E: Four drawing operations on paper

Creative drawing, four lessons. These are narrower extensions of the drawing foundations, not claims that the existing curriculum lacks observation or composition.

### 17. Match an ellipse to its tilt

**Distinct skill:** Control ellipse openness and symmetry when depicting a circular face. Check the relationship between the narrow and wide spans rather than drawing a habitual oval.

**Practice, 9 minutes:** Hold a plain circular lid near the center of your view at three tilts. Make one small drawing per tilt. Mark each ellipse's widest and narrowest spans and compare their ratio by eye. Correct the drawing whose opening most disagrees with the observed lid. Keep the viewpoint steady and use any comfortable pencil grip.

**Source check:** S21, [Lesson 1: Ellipses](https://drawabox.com/lesson/1/ellipses), Drawabox Art Instruction Inc. The browser-rendered "Circles in 3D space," "Degree," and "Minor axis" sections were read after the initial page-loading screen. They connect ellipse shape to a circle's orientation and define its axes.

**Limitation:** This is an introductory near-center construction check, not a full treatment of projective geometry. Do not generalize simplified axis alignments to every perspective setup or require Drawabox's pen, homework volume, or motor technique.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `design-038`, "Build a subject from volumes you can turn," already uses a cylindrical mug, centerline, and rim. The added operation is a dedicated ellipse check across tilt, not another cylinder construction. Classify it as underdeveloped, not absent.

### 18. Shade with crossed lines

**Distinct skill:** Build tonal differences with layers of marks and use mark direction to describe a surface when smooth blending is unavailable or unwanted.

**Practice, 10 minutes:** Use a ballpoint or pencil to make four small swatches, increasing line density or adding a crossing layer while avoiding heavier pressure as the only change. Then shade a small drawing of a rolled paper strip with those marks. Curve one group of strokes with the form and compare it with an equally dark flat swatch.

**Source check:** S22, Thom Dower, [Make Art @ Home: Welcome to Crosshatching](https://unframed.lacma.org/2020/07/06/make-art-home%E2%80%94welcome-crosshatching), Los Angeles County Museum of Art, Unframed, 6 July 2020. The single-line, rounded-crosshatching, and density stages explain layered tone and directional marks. Link text uses plain punctuation; the source URL is unchanged.

**Limitation:** More marks can obscure a light area, and different pens and papers behave differently. A crossing angle is a craft choice, not a universal number. Do not copy the source photograph or repeat its tool-size specification.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `design-042`, "Group the shadows before shading the texture," establishes a value plan. This proposal supplies a specific mark-making method for producing that plan with line-only media.

### 19. Suggest texture without outlining everything

**Distinct skill:** Imply small surface forms through selected cast-shadow shapes, controlling detail density without outlining every ridge or repeating a texture symbol.

**Practice, 10 minutes:** Examine ridged cardboard under side light. Draw two small patches: one outlining each visible ridge, the other showing only selected observed shadow shapes. Compare which patch retains the surface relationship with fewer marks. Mark a doubtful dark patch as uncertain instead of assuming every dark area is a shadow.

**Source check:** S23, [Lesson 2: Texture](https://drawabox.com/lesson/2/2), Drawabox Art Instruction Inc. Browser-rendered sections "Cast shadows," "Implicit vs explicit," and "Detail density" explain the deliberate use and omission of shadow shapes. The direct fetch initially returned a loading screen, so it was not counted until the body was read in the browser.

**Limitation:** Surface color, reflection, and form shading are not cast shadows. Restricting a study to shadow shapes is a course exercise, not a claim that all texture should be drawn that way. Do not copy the site's gradients, examples, or claims about a fixed order of visual processing.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `design-047`, "Observe how a material handles light," compares material appearances, while `design-044` identifies cast shadows. This proposal adds selective mark omission as a way to represent texture and manage competing detail.

### 20. Scale a sketch with a grid

**Distinct skill:** Transfer an existing composition at a changed size while preserving relative positions. Distinguish scaling a drawing from inventing a new layout.

**Practice, 12 minutes:** Draw an original leaf silhouette in a small rectangle divided into four rows and four columns. Draw a second rectangle twice as wide and twice as high, with the same cell count. Transfer the contour by its crossings and positions inside corresponding cells. Check three landmarks before adding any detail. Keep the first sketch visible.

**Source check:** S24, Annette Wickham, [Art history 101: what is a cartoon?](https://www.royalacademy.org.uk/article/daniel-maclise-what-is-a-cartoon), Royal Academy of Arts, 1 September 2015. "So, what's this 'squaring' method?" explains transferring a design using pencil grids and identifies a preparatory example. Relevant article body was read.

**Limitation:** A grid transfers mistakes as readily as correct relationships. Changing horizontal and vertical scale by different factors distorts the image. Historical examples are context, not assets to reproduce; this drill uses the learner's own silhouette.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `design-019`, "Use shared alignment to organize a page," uses a layout grid; `design-046` compares composition thumbnails. This operation preserves a chosen thumbnail during enlargement, rather than arranging content along alignment guides.

## Pack F: UI behavior beyond the screenshot

Creative UI design, four lessons. Paper exercises produce specifications to test later. They do not certify an interface's implementation or WCAG conformance.

### 21. Let speech match the label

**Distinct skill:** Distinguish a visible control label from its computed accessible name and keep the visible wording usable as a speech command.

**Practice, 8 minutes:** On paper, specify three controls for a sketch gallery. Beside each visible label, write the intended accessible name. Include a visible "Save sketch" paired with the mismatching name "Add artwork to collection," then repair it without losing needed context. Check whether the visible text occurs intact and in order in each name.

**Source check:** S25, [Understanding SC 2.5.3: Label in Name](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/label-in-name.html), W3C WAI. The Level A criterion requires the name to contain the visible text label. The intent explains speech interaction and warns that ARIA names can override visible labels. Starting the name with the label is a best practice, not an additional mandatory requirement.

**Limitation:** This criterion has scope rules, including controls without visible text labels. Matching strings alone does not establish the computed name, correct semantics, keyboard access, or speech software behavior. Test the actual control later.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `design-026`, "Make possible actions discoverable," concerns signifiers a newcomer can perceive. This adds consistency between visible language and the programmatic control name.

### 22. Give a dialog a return route

**Distinct skill:** Specify focus as a lifecycle: entry into a modal, movement within it, dismissal, and a useful destination after it closes.

**Practice, 10 minutes:** Draw a gallery, an "Edit title" opener, and its modal. Move a paper token through the planned initial focus, each Tab stop, the close action, and the return target. Remove the opener card as a second scenario and choose a logical remaining destination. State that background controls are unavailable while this modal is active.

**Source check:** S26, [Dialog (Modal) Pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/), W3C ARIA Authoring Practices Guide. "Keyboard Interaction" and its notes specify containment, Escape, content-dependent initial focus, return to the invoking element, and exceptions when that element disappears or workflow changes.

**Limitation:** APG is pattern guidance, not a standalone WCAG conformance test. Merely labeling an overlay modal does not implement its behavior. Initial focus should fit the content and consequences; it is not always the first input or the dialog container.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `design-035`, "Give the goal without giving away the control," tests discoverability. This proposal designs the navigation state across opening and closing a modal, which paper screen appearance and a successful tap do not establish.

### 23. Keep meaning without motion

**Distinct skill:** Preserve an interaction's information when optional animation is disabled. Separate a state change from the movement used to illustrate it.

**Practice, 9 minutes:** Sketch a saved-item interaction that normally moves a card into a collection. Make a second specification with no travel, zoom, or bounce: the state updates and a stationary message names the destination. Compare whether both versions communicate the same result and preserve the next action. Do not create or watch a provocative motion demo to test sensitivity.

**Source check:** S27, [Understanding SC 2.3.3: Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html), W3C WAI. The Level AAA criterion requires that interaction-triggered motion animation can be disabled unless it is essential to functionality or the information conveyed. Its intent distinguishes this from automatically started motion and describes controls or user preferences.

**Limitation:** Do not mislabel this AAA criterion as AA, or assume that making movement faster solves the problem. Essential motion and automatic animation have separate considerations. A design plan still needs testing with actual preference support and assistive technology.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `design-027`, "Show what happened after the tap," mentions feedback beyond color or motion. This makes the nonmoving equivalent its own design operation and specifies when it is used.

### 24. Reserve space before images arrive

**Distinct skill:** Design the loading state so late media does not unexpectedly move a control or reading position. Distinguish visual stability from loading speed.

**Practice, 10 minutes:** Draw a phone card before and after its 4:3 sketch preview loads, including title and Save control. First let the preview appear with no reserved space and mark the control's movement. Redraw the before-state with the intended aspect-ratio space already present. Compare the positions while allowing text to wrap, rather than fixing the whole card height.

**Source check:** S28, [Optimize Cumulative Layout Shift](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls), Google web.dev, by Addy Osmani and Barry Pollard. "Images without dimensions" and "Reserve space for late-loading content" explain advance space allocation; the lab/field discussion notes that shifts can occur after initial load.

**Limitation:** A placeholder does not speed a download or solve font changes and every later update. Do not clip larger text to protect a fixed rectangle. A screenshot or paper exercise cannot measure CLS, and a low metric does not prove every interaction is comfortable.

**Nearest existing overlap and why new:** `design-024`, "Test awkward content before polishing the sample," tests long content and spacing overrides. This adds a time-dependent layout problem: what changes between loading and loaded states.
