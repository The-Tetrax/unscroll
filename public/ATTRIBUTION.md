# UNSCROLL assets and content

The app, original teaching guides, exercises, explanatory diagrams, and original Ink fox artwork are provided under the project's MIT license. Character and film names are used only for factual commentary and learning references. The original content contains no franchise artwork, copyrighted film stills, or copyrighted speech excerpts. Pictures by other people are a separate group with their own section below. Among them, film frames are used only where the source page marks the film as public domain or freely licensed, and a few photographs show someone else's work in passing, on a screen or on a product.

## Fonts
- Barlow Condensed, designed by Jeremy Tribby and contributors. SIL Open Font License 1.1. Distributed locally through @fontsource/barlow-condensed. The full license is in licenses/barlow-condensed-OFL.txt.
- DM Sans, designed by Colophon Foundry and contributors. SIL Open Font License 1.1. Distributed locally through @fontsource/dm-sans. The full license is in licenses/dm-sans-OFL.txt.

asset-licenses.json contains the source file, license, byte size, and SHA-256 for each bundled font, generated icon, and all 604 teaching diagrams. The build refreshes these records and the test suite verifies them.

## Teaching illustrations
All teaching SVGs in lesson-visuals are original explanatory artwork. There are 97 bespoke primary diagrams: camera, light/editing, color/type, UI/UX, drawing, and the two approved sample illustrations. Other diagrams are generated from lesson-specific values, relationships, and dialogue. Hypothetical figures are not empirical results, drawings are not photographs, and visual layouts do not establish live interface behavior or accessibility conformance. SVG title/description text is retained alongside readable captions and HTML detail lists.

## Pictures by other people
Some lessons show a photograph, an artwork, a frame from a public-domain film, a scan of a printed or handwritten page, or a diagram made by someone else. A few are memory hooks: one of these pictures with a line of original UNSCROLL text set under it, not into it. These files are in lesson-photos. They are not original UNSCROLL artwork and are not covered by the MIT license. Each one is in the public domain or under a Creative Commons CC0, Attribution, or Attribution-ShareAlike license, as declared on its Wikimedia Commons page when the file was added. That declaration is the uploader's and has not been verified independently. Each picture was resized or cropped as its record states and converted to WebP, and a faint scan may have had its contrast raised, which its record then says. The credit under each picture names its author and license and links to its source. lesson-photos/CREDITS.md lists every file with its work title, author, license, source and changes, and src/teaching/photos.json holds the same records with byte counts and SHA-256 values that the tests verify. A credit does not mean that the author endorses UNSCROLL. Captions are original UNSCROLL text.

Where a record says public domain, that is the status given on the file's Commons page. For some files it rests on the rules of one country, and the status elsewhere can differ. In the United States these are publication without a copyright notice, as for the drawing from a bicycle manual of 1977, a copyright that was not renewed, authorship by a federal agency, and the view that a faithful photograph or scan of an old painting, print, banknote or record earns no copyright of its own. A few files rest on the rules of the country where they were first published, such as the terms for older photographs in Japan and Finland. For the film-set photograph of 1939 in one cinema lesson, the page rests on the general practice of film studios of the time and names no search of the renewal records for this photograph. Museums and libraries that hold old works often publish their own photographs or scans under terms of their own. The British Museum, the source of the Rembrandt drawing in one design lesson, offers images from its website under a non-commercial Creative Commons license and asks for a separate license for commercial use. The Imperial War Museums released a War Office photograph of 1942 under their own non-commercial license, and Commons marks it public domain because British Crown copyright in it has expired. Commons treats a faithful reproduction of a public-domain original as public domain, and the records here follow Commons.

Notes on single files:
- The banknote in one money lesson was scanned by the Commons user Godot13 from the National Numismatic Collection of the National Museum of American History. Its page marks the note public domain and also places the scan under CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/), for countries where a scan has a copyright of its own. The credit under the picture names the issuer, the scanner and the collection.
- The photograph of US Airways Flight 1549 was under CC BY 2.0 on Flickr when an upload bot copied it to Commons. Its Flickr page has changed license since, which Commons records. A Creative Commons license is not withdrawn from copies made while it applied.
- A screenshot can contain other works. The editing timeline in one cinema lesson shows two small video frames, the first on a clip named after PRISM: Snowden Interview by Laura Poitras and Praxis Films, a film that Commons hosts under a Creative Commons license. Its source page does not say where the second frame comes from.
- A photograph can show other works. The gift card in one money lesson is cropped from a photograph of a shop rack, whose Commons page treats the printed card designs as incidental. The small pictures on that card are blurred in the source file, and the neighboring cards, some with sharp artwork, are cropped away. The monitors in the dubbing studio of one cinema lesson show a frame of a cartoon. Photographs of products show their makers' names, logos and packaging. A photograph published by the FBI shows a scene sketch drawn by school students in one of its programs. Its public-domain mark is the one for the FBI's photograph.

## Icons
Lucide icons use the ISC license, available in the lucide-react package. They are bundled as inline SVG components. The custom fox brand artwork is original UNSCROLL artwork, not a franchise character.

## External discovery text
Downloaded Wikipedia extracts are attributed to Wikipedia contributors and licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0. Each extract retains its source article, history link, license link, and edition date. Extracts may be shortened and formatted as plain text. Their license does not change when included in a local backup.

Original lessons summarize linked references in original words. A linked source does not imply that its author endorses UNSCROLL or tested the app's exercises.
